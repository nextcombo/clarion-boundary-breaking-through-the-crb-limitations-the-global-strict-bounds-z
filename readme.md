Among many known lower bounds, the Cramér-Rao Bound (CRB), widely accepted due to its locality, can only constrain the MSE in the asymptotic region; whereas the Ziv-Zakai Bound (ZZB), less frequently used, possesses global tightness but is limited by the single-source assumption. Introduction: Direction of Arrival (DOA) estimation is a fundamental problem in numerous array processing applications such as radar, sonar, navigation, and wireless communication [1]. Over the past few decades, DOA estimation from multiple sources has attracted extensive research attention, with most studies focusing on algorithm design. Typical algorithms include Multiple Signal Classification (MUSIC), Rotation Invariant Techniques for Signal Parameter Estimation (ESPRIT), Sparse Basis Algorithms, and Machine Learning-Based Algorithms [2-4].
Mean Squared Error (MSE) is a common metric for evaluating the performance of estimators. However, as a typical nonlinear parameter estimation problem, direction of arrival (DOA) estimation does not have an exact closed-form minimum Mean Squared Error solution, thus urgently necessitating the search for lower bounds on MSE (e.g., see [14] and its references). Among them, the Cramér-Rao Bound (CRB) [15] is widely used due to its ease of calculation, with its closed-form expression given by the inverse of the Fisher Information Matrix [16,17]. To address the singularity issue of the Fisher Information Matrix under underdetermined conditions, [18,19] proposed the covariance array CRB by deriving a non-singular Fisher Information Matrix based on a covariance array. These CRBs treat DOA as an unknown deterministic parameter, constraining the MSE for any specific parameter value. However, this characteristic limits their use of prior information about the parameter space, resulting in the CRBs only being tight in the asymptotic region.
To overcome the locality of classical CRB, Many Bayesian bounds have been proposed in the past decades [14]. Typical examples include bounds based on the covariance inequality [20]. Bayesian CRB (BCRB) [1] Weiss-Winterbau (WWB) [21] Lu Wen-Meisel border (RMB) [22] Bayes-Abel Bound (RAB) [23] and Bobrow-Zakaie Bound (BZB) [24]. Although these boundaries may provide more stringent constraints, But they rely on higher-order derivatives. Free variable optimization and empirical test point selection [14].
As another typical Bayesian bound, the Ziv-Zakai Bound (ZZB) provides a global tightness for mean squared error within a wide signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) range. The ZZB was initially proposed in [25] for scalar estimation problems under the assumption of uniform prior distributions for delays. Subsequently, research on the ZZB for specific scalar parameter estimation problems has been extensively conducted [28-35], such as single-source direction of arrival estimation based on linear arrays [28,30]. On the other hand, [36] first derived a unified expression for vector parameter estimation ZZB applicable to any prior distribution, providing a feasible framework for deriving ZZB for direction of arrival vector estimation.However, Due to the high-dimensional integration and constraint optimization, The derivation of explicit ZZB still faces challenges. Although [37] derives the explicit ZZB for two-dimensional wave arrival direction estimation, However, the Sherman-Morrison formula and the matrix determinant lemma make it only applicable to single-source scenarios; Furthermore, It assumes that sin θ follows a uniform distribution rather than the direction of arrival θ itself. This is not realistic in actual wavefront estimation - the assumption means that the signal enters from the visual axis direction with the highest probability. And the probability of deviation from the line of sight gradually decreases.
Therefore, the existing single-source direction estimation ZZB is difficult to extend to multi-source scenarios, mainly facing the following challenges:
1  
As the number of sources increases, the computational burden for numerical solutions significantly increases, necessitating explicit ZZB solutions. However, the existing explicit ZZB derived from the Sherman-Morrison formula and matrix determinant lemma is only applicable to single source direction estimation.
2  
The natural presence of permutation ambiguity in the mean square error calculation for multi-source direction estimation. The sorting operation to eliminate the ambiguity of displacement blur will implicitly change the prior distribution of the wave arrival direction. And affect the mean square error of wave arrival direction estimation outside the asymptotic region. This results in the ZZB being invalid in the region based on existing framework deductions.
3  
Due to multipath propagation, the cross-correlation between coherent sources is prevalent in multi-source direction estimation (see [38-40] and references therein), but its impact on ZZB remains unclear.
In response to these challenges, this paper investigates the derivation of ZZB for mixed coherent/incoherent multi-source direction estimation: Firstly, utilizing the Woodbury matrix identity and Sylvester determinant theorem, the ZZB for single-source direction estimation is extended to multi-source scenarios based on the framework derived in [37]. However, this direct extension does not account for the permutation ambiguity in the mean square error calculation of multi-source direction estimation, leading to its failure in the prior performance region. To address this, this paper introduces order statistics to describe the impact of permutation ambiguity on ZZB, ensuring that the derived ZZB can still tightly constrain the mean square error of direction estimation devices outside of the asymptotic region.The derived ZZB is an explicit function of the number of sources, array sensors, quick-looks, prior distributions of sources, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), array observation data, and coherence coefficients. It provides a unified expression for both overdetermined and underdetermined direction of arrival (DOA) estimation, and clearly reveals the relationship between the mean square error convergence in the prior performance region and the number of sources. Simulation results show that, thanks to significant threshold effects, the derived ZZB can effectively predict the performance of multi-source DOA estimation; compared with the widely used CRB, this ZZB offers tighter bounds within a wider range of SNR, and clearly reveals the impact of coherence coefficients in the transition region of the prior performance region.
Method Introduction
1. Signal Model
Assume K distant narrow-band signals are incident onto a linear array of M sensors, consisting of L (1≤L≤K) interfering sources and the remaining K-L non-interfering sources that are independent of the first L signals. The array observation data is modeled as:
x(t) = A(θ)s(t) + n(t),∀t=1,2,…,T
where A(θ)=[a(θ₁),a(θ₂),...,a(θ_K)]∈ℂ^(M×K) is the steering matrix, a(θₖ) is the steering vector for the kth source (λ is the wavelength); s(t) is the signal vector, containing the coherence coefficient β (β₁=1, representing the correlation between the coherent signal and reference signal s₁(t)); n(t)~CN(0_M,σₙ²I_M) is zero-mean additive Gaussian noise, which is independent of the signals; T is the number of samples.
2. Core Method Derivation (1) Generalized ZZB for Multi-Source Coupled Coherent/Non-Coherent Scenarios
To abandon the Sherman-Morrison formula and matrix determinant lemma applicable to single-source scenarios, adopt Woodbury matrix identities and Sylvester determinant theorems. Based on [37], the ZZB is extended from single-source to multi-source by Taylor expansion of the semi-invariant moment generating function in the ZZB derivation. This leads to an approximate expression P_min(φ,φ+δ) suitable for multi-source scenarios (separated into P_S(δ) within Δ and P_L outside the region), thus deriving the generalized ZZB for multi-source:
MSE ≥ [12P_LTr{R_θ}]/[(K+1)(K+2)] + Γ_(3/2)(ũ)[Tr{J⁻¹}]/K
where R_θ is the prior covariance matrix, J is the Fisher information matrix, and Γ_(3/2)(·) is the normalized incomplete gamma function.
(2) In the calculation of mean square error (MSE) for the improved multi-source direction estimation with ZZB considering permutation ambiguity, the sorting operation (used to eliminate permutation ambiguity) indirectly changes the prior distribution of direction of arrival (DOA), leading to the failure of generalized ZZB outside the asymptotic region. To address this issue, order statistics are introduced, defining the sorted DOA $\theta_k$ (the kth smallest order statistic), and deriving its prior variance $\sigma_{\theta_k}^2$. Consequently, a scaling factor $\kappa = \frac{2}{K+1}$ is derived (related to the number of sources K). This scaling factor is incorporated into the generalized ZZB, resulting in the final expression for ZZB:
$$ MSE \geq \frac{2P_L[K\zeta^2]}{[(K+1)^2(K+2)]} + \Gamma_{3/2}(\hat{\nu})[\text{Tr}\{J^{-1}\}]/K $$
where $\zeta$ represents the range of DOA values. This expression converges to the prior bound (APB) in the low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) prior performance region and to the Cramer-Rao bound (CRB) in the high SNR asymptotic region, achieving global tight constraints.
(3) Unified over-determined and under-determined estimation addresses the under-determined DOA estimation (K > M) by using the covariance Fisher information matrix (avoiding rank deficiency issues), ensuring that the derived ZZB can be uniformly applicable across over-determined and under-determined scenarios.
Conclusion The paper derives an explicit ZZB suitable for mixed coherent/non-coherent multi-source DOA estimation, which is a function of source numbers, array sensor counts, number of samples, prior distribution of sources, SNR, array observation data, and coherence coefficients. Unlike single-source DOA estimation, the sorting operation used to eliminate permutation ambiguity in the mean square error calculation for multi-source scenarios results in the generalized ZZB failing outside the asymptotic region. Therefore, this paper introduces order statistics to describe the impact of this sorting operation on ZZB, enabling the derived ZZB to globally constrain the mean square error for multi-source DOA estimation. The proposed ZZB provides a unified bound for both over-determined and under-determined DOA estimation, and for the first time reveals the relationship between mean square error convergence performance and source numbers (unrelated to number of samples and linear array configuration). Simulation results show that the derived ZZB has significant advantages over CRB in a wider range of SNR.
Future work will study when a part of the source is close to distribution. The impact of Feisil information on the transitional area ZZB. Furthermore, Although this paper derives ZZB for direction of arrival estimation in linear array, However, the core conclusion is expected to be extended to problems of two-dimensional wave arrival direction estimation and other discrete vector parameter estimations with permutation ambiguity.
